In the forestless valley the chief trees were the date palm, the syeamore, tamarisk and acacia, none of which furnished good timber. Wood was therefore scarce and expensive, but the carpenters, joiners and cabinet makers flourished never- theless, and those in the employ of the palace or on the estates of the nobles wrought wonders in the cedar, imported from Syria, and the ebony and ivory which came in from the south. We shall find these shipwrights building the earliest known sea-going vessels, on the shores of the Red Sea. While the artistic craftsman in stone still produced mag- nificent vessels, vases, jars, bowls and platters in alabaster, diorite, porphyry and other costly stones, yet his work was gradually giving way to the potter, whose rich blue- and green-glazed fayence vessels could not but win their way.
