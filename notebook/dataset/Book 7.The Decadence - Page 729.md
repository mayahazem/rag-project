Hgypt had now been under the divided authority of nu- merous local dynasts for probably over a century and a half. The total disintegration of centralized power had unavoidably involved the sacrifice of economic prosperity. Kgypt’s foreign commerce inevitably dwindled to the van- ishing point; agriculture and industry were at the lowest ebb and the resources of the country, at the mercy of irre- sponsible lords and princes, were necessarily being rapidly drained. With its vast works of irrigation slowly going to ruin, its roads unprotected, intercourse between cities unsafe and the larger communities suffering from constant turmoil and agitation, the productive capacity of the country was steadily waning.
