Close to each pyramid on the hither side is the pyramid-temple. From two of these, covered masonry causeways lead down to the edge of the desert plateau, where each terminates in a monumental gate of massive masonry (see Fig. 69). Before the gate is a landing platform with steps leading down to the water, where boats may land during the inundation. All this was on the edge of the plateau overlooking the valley, in which, below the pyramid, there now grew up a walled town. Leading up from the town to the pyramid enclosure was a massive causeway of stone which terminated at the lower or townward end in a large and stately struc- ture of granite or limestone sometimes with floors of alabas- ter, the whole forming a superb portal, a worthy entrance to so impressive a tomb (Figs. 35, 69). Through this portal passed the white-robed procession on feast days, moving from the town up the long white causeway to the temple, above which rose the mighty mass of the pyramid.
